# ARPA v0.9.1 Adversarial Hardening

ARPA v0.9.1 is a Candidate Specification hardening increment over the v0.9.0 architecture. It does not redesign ARPA. It closes ambiguity that could otherwise permit divergent or adversarially permissive authority evaluation.

## Normative changes

- define downstream authority as a monotonic semantic intersection of upstream authority and child-declared scope;
- preserve omitted constraints and mandatory prohibitions through delegation;
- define half-open validity intervals and explicit clock/event-ordering failure behavior;
- constrain `not_applicable` so it cannot represent failure to establish required authority;
- require non-affirmative outcomes for unresolved conflicts between simultaneously competent authoritative sources;
- separate the immediate authority effect of revocation from enforcement-convergence evidence;
- make the complete evaluation time/checkpoint state part of deterministic decision reproducibility;
- require proof-input semantics in addition to canonicalization;
- define monotonic composition of independent status dimensions;
- require an authorized continuity mechanism for pairwise/scoped identifiers; and
- prevent schema-only corrections from acquiring de facto normative authority.

## Executable assurance

The release adds `conformance/test-vectors/adversarial/adversarial-authority-v0.9.1.json` with more than twenty boundary cases and `registries/adversarial-hardening-requirements-v0.9.1.json` mapping the amendment to verification and evidence expectations. `scripts/validate_candidate_hardening.py` gates vector structure, prohibited affirmative outcomes, `not_applicable` bypass prevention, and requirement-to-vector traceability.

## IETF synchronization

Protocol-core hardening semantics are represented in `ietf/fragments/adversarial-hardening.md`. `scripts/build_ietf_draft.sh` deterministically inserts the fragment before the unnumbered Internet-Draft back matter and regenerates RFCXML v3, plaintext and HTML.

The dedicated IETF GitHub Actions workflow runs automatically for IETF source/build changes. The Pages workflow independently rebuilds and validates the same IETF publication surface before deployment. Generated files remain derivative artifacts and are not committed to Git.

## Assurance boundary

These fixtures and repository-controlled implementations provide candidate evidence for the modeled cases. They are not independent certification, formal verification, cryptographic review, or proof of production security.
